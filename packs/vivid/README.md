# vivid

Ornaments drawn as SVG for portolani, after public-domain originals: drawn, not traced. Each sprite's
`after` in `pack.json` names the page it is after; each file's opening comment says what it shows.
Paper shows through white, since the renderer lays sprites on the parchment with `multiply`.

## How to read a rose

Each rose style is a pair: a centre rose (300 px) and a small rose for the ring (110 px), drawn to match.

- **aguiar**: after Jorge de Aguiar's chart, 1492, with the small roses of the Catalan Atlas, 1375. The four lapis points are the cardinals and a black cross marks the east; on the small rose a gilt pole star marks the north.
- **martines**: after Joan Martines of Messina, the chart of 1584 at Trinity College Cambridge and the atlas of 1591. North is a black fleur-de-lis with a green bar; a black cross in the lettered disc marks the east, and the other winds carry their initials (G, S, O, L, P, M for Greco, Scirocco, Ostro, Libeccio, Ponente, Maestro). The small rose's north is its black spike and the arrowhead at its centre.
- **oliva**: after Salvator Oliva's atlas, Marseille 1619. North is a blue fleur-de-lis; the other three cardinals are gilt points with ink dots. East has no mark of its own.
- **brouscon**: after the centre rose of Guillaume Brouscon's almanac, Le Conquet 1543. The four black-and-blue points are the cardinals; nothing singles out north or east, as on the original.
- **fleurs**: after the upper right rose of the same Brouscon almanac. Every one of the eight winds wears a gilt fleur-de-lis, so no mark tells north from the rest; read it by its place on the chart.
- **dieppe**: after the anonymous Dieppe atlas of 1547 (Huntington HM 29). North is the gilt fleur-de-lis outside the ring; a string of beads off the ring marks the east. The original chart is south-up; these are drawn north-up.
