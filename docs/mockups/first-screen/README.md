# Mockups: the first screen and the landing view

Three first screens a stranger could open on, each with its own landing view, beside the page as it stands. Each candidate is a phone (390 × 844) and a desktop (1280 × 800), in one picture; `overview.png` stacks all four. The candidates are drawn over the live page by `shoot.mjs` (DOM and CSS added after the map paints) and are not wired into the page. In every candidate the timing, memento and build lines leave the foot of the map (they would move to the Configuration drawer's foot).

0. `live.png`: the page today. The Tyrrhenian Sea at the default view; the only words are the timing line, the memento mori and the last commit's subject, overprinting each other at the foot on a phone; the name and "drag to pan" sit inside the Configuration drawer.
1. `candidate-1.png`, a title plate over the Atlantic rose. A parchment plate in the upper third, in the chart's own type: PORTOLANI, "The world as a 15th-century portolan chart", drag · pinch · tap, "tap anywhere to begin"; the first touch puts it away and it does not come back. The view lands on the wind rose off Iberia, Biscay and the Channel at the top, Portugal and the Azores below.
2. `candidate-2.png`, the cartouche names it; Mansa Musa to tap. No plate: the cartouche card reads PORTOLANI with "the world as a 15th-century portolan chart" lettered under it, and a dark ribbon at the foot says drag · pinch · tap until the first drag. The view lands on the Catalan Atlas laid over West Africa, Mansa Musa enthroned at the centre with a ring and "tap a figure for its story" on him; the ring goes at the first tap. Costs the atlas's sheet on first load.
3. `candidate-3.png`, a legend card; Columbus crosses as it opens. A card at the lower left, beside the scale on a desktop and above it on a phone, with the name, one sentence on what the map is, the drag · pinch · tap line and "What a portolan chart was →"; its ✕ puts it away. The view lands on the North Atlantic with Columbus's first crossing drawing itself (2.5 s), the Sargasso rose and the landfall in view.
